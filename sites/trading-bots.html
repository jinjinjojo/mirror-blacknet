synopsis

The short version

The bots that make the most money are market makers. Citadel Securities ($12.2B trading revenue in 2025), XTX Markets (£1.71B profit) and Virtu (one losing day in 1,238) quote both sides of the market and collect the spread on enormous volume. They predict just well enough to avoid getting picked off. Renaissance shows the other route: thousands of tiny statistical edges, right maybe 50.75% of the time, repeated until luck averages out.

On public blockchains the edge is position. We decoded jaredfromsubway.eth, the bot behind most Ethereum sandwiches. Its contract only accepts calls from its owner, only in the exact second they were planned for (msg.value must equal the block timestamp's low byte), and dispatches through a 256-slot jump table with no ABI at all. In two live sandwiches it kept about 108,000 wei each. The block builder got the rest.

For retail bots the reliable money is the toll, not the trading. Axiom's bundle contains the exact 1% fee function and three referral levels. Maestro's router has an owner-only changeFee(uint16). MQL5 keeps 20% of every forex signal subscription. Trade Ideas sells Holly for $127–254 a month with hand-picked winners hard-coded into its homepage. When frontier LLMs traded real money in Alpha Arena, 29 of 32 runs lost money after fees.

Around all of it sits a hope business: a $1.7B bot Ponzi with no bot, fake MEV-bot contracts that drain the people who deploy them, AI-washing fines, and 95%-win-rate robots that blow up.
