lab 2 · market maker

Earning the spread, losing to the informed

A market maker quotes both sides around the price. Random traders pay it the spread. Traders who know where the price is going take money from it. The mix decides everything.

expected / trade
$0.02
break-even informed share
60%
P&L, your flow
$31
P&L, retail-only flow
$50

Each tick one trader arrives. Noise traders pick a side at random and pay you half the spread. Informed traders know the next $0.05 move and pick you off. Expected profit per trade = spread/2 − informed share × move. That formula is why wholesalers pay brokers for retail order flow (the blue line) and why market makers widen or pull quotes when smart money shows up.

$10,000 account, 1,000 trades a year, each trade 49% to win after the spread. The robot doubles its lot after each loss so the next win recovers everything. Almost every sequence ends in a small win, until a streak of losses needs a lot bigger than the account.

How to make a perfect backtest from nothing

A pure random market, moving-average crossover strategies with random settings, and one rule: keep whichever backtest looks best.

The market here is a pure coin-flip random walk, so no moving-average crossover has a real edge. Try more settings and the best backtest keeps improving, while what happens afterwards stays a coin flip. Selling the best of many backtests is how many “proven” robots are made.
