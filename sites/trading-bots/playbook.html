9 plays for building or using a bot

LEGITIMATEP1Measure the edge after costs before automating

Write down expected win rate, average win, average loss and all-in cost per trade. If expected value per trade is not clearly above cost, automation only loses faster. The edge machine on the simulators page does this math.

requires

A trade log or paper-trading record of a few hundred trades.

expected effect

Avoids the most common failure mode: a real but tiny edge eaten by fees.

legality

Fine everywhere.

LEGITIMATEP2Cut costs before chasing smarter signals

Limit orders instead of market orders, fewer trades, low-fee venues, no 1% platform tolls. At small edges, cost is the whole game: Virtu's model works because its costs are near zero.

requires

Discipline and a fee-aware venue.

expected effect

Often the single largest improvement available to a retail bot.

legality

Fine everywhere.

LEGITIMATEP3Copy the pros' risk structure, not their speed

Fixed-fraction position sizing, a hard daily loss limit, a kill switch, and never adding to a loser. Knight Capital shows what happens when one control fails.

requires

Code that enforces limits outside the strategy logic.

expected effect

Turns blow-ups into bad days.

legality

Fine everywhere.

LEGITIMATEP4Use LLMs as analysts, not autopilots

Alpha Arena shows the same prompt producing +22% for one model and −63% for another. Let a model summarise and propose; let fixed rules decide size and exits.

requires

Hard-coded risk caps the model can't override.

expected effect

Gets the research speed without handing leverage to a coin flip.

legality

Fine everywhere.

AMBIGUOUSP5Provide liquidity on DEXs

Earn swap fees by supplying liquidity. Expect impermanent loss when prices trend, and JIT bots like jared that jump in front of the best trades for a single block.

requires

Capital, monitoring, and understanding of concentrated liquidity ranges.

expected effect

Fees can beat holding in sideways markets and lose in trending ones.

legality

Legal; tax treatment varies.

AMBIGUOUSP6Run an MEV searcher

Back-run arbitrage and liquidations are the non-harmful kinds. Margins have been competed to near zero: our two decoded jared sandwiches netted ~108,000 wei each after paying the builder.

requires

Low-latency infrastructure, builder relationships, solid Solidity/Yul.

expected effect

Thin and competitive. Sandwiching harms users and carries legal risk.

legality

Arbitrage and liquidations are generally legal; sandwiching is contested.

DEFENSIVEP7Don't get sandwiched

Send swaps through a private or MEV-protected RPC, keep slippage tolerance tight, and split large trades in thin pools. Axiom turns this on by default with Jito's 'jitodontfront' marker.

requires

Wallet RPC setting or a protected-send option.

expected effect

Removes the price impact a sandwich bot would charge you.

legality

Fine everywhere.

DEFENSIVEP8Lock down bot API keys

Trade-only permission, IP allow-list, a dedicated sub-account with limited funds, and rotation. The 3Commas leak hurt people whose keys could do more than they needed to.

requires

Five minutes in the exchange's API settings.

expected effect

A leaked key can't empty the account.

legality

Fine everywhere.

DEFENSIVEP9Vet any bot before paying

Run the red-flag checker. Ask for a live, third-party-verified record on real capital, with costs. Ask why it's for sale: Medallion stopped taking outside money once it worked.

requires

Skepticism.

expected effect

Filters out the bulk of scams and hype products.

legality

Fine everywhere.
