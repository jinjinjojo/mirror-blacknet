six engines

Where trading-bot money comes from

Every profitable bot sits on at least one of these engines. Each has a payer. If you can't name who pays a bot's profits, the honest answer is usually “its users”. Pick an engine to see the flow.

Market making

Buy at $99.99, sell at $100.01, millions of times a day.

who pays

Anyone who wants to trade right now. A market order pays the gap between the best buy and sell price.

mechanism

Quote both sides of the book at once, earn the gap when both fill, hedge the leftover inventory within milliseconds, and pull quotes when the incoming flow looks informed.

can a retail bot do this?

Very hard. You would be quoting against firms that see the order book microseconds earlier and pay lower fees, so you get filled mostly when you are wrong.

real example

Virtu's IPO filing: one losing day in 1,238 trading days, earning "small bid/ask spreads on large trading volumes".

the pattern

Prediction is the smallest part

Structure beats forecasting

The biggest earners (Citadel Securities, Virtu, XTX) are paid to provide liquidity. Their forecasts mainly decide where to quote and when to step back, not which way to bet.

Edges are tiny, volume is huge

A 0.75-point edge over a coin flip is a fortune at 50,000 trades a day with near-zero costs, and nothing at 10 trades a day with retail fees. The edge machine on the simulators page shows the crossover.

Retail bots run on tolls

Memecoin terminals take 1% per trade, signal sellers take subscriptions, robot sellers take $300–$1,000 a copy. Those revenues don't depend on the user winning.
