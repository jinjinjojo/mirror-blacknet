synopsis

One-page summary

A crypto casino is a betting ledger with a markup. Every game pays a little under fair odds: about 1% on in-house originals, 3–12% on slots, 20–30% on parlays. Over millions of bets that markup is gross gaming revenue. Out of it come rakeback and bonuses (20–40%), slot studios (10–20% of their games' GGR), the white-label platform if it's rented (10–30%), affiliates (20–60% of net), streamers and sponsorships, payment fees and staff. A small rented casino keeps around 6% of GGR. Stake's tech parent reported a 26% net margin.

The big operators build the parts that hold the margin: the wallet ledger, originals, rewards engine and risk rules. Stake runs SvelteKit with 112 GraphQL operations, Shuffle Next.js on Vercel with ~350, BC.Game SolidJS with protobuf over socket.io. They rent everything else: slots through Hub88 or SoftSwiss, sportsbooks from Betby, ID checks from Veriff, Sumsub or Onfido, crypto on-ramps from MoonPay or Swapped. Smaller brands like BitStarz and 7Bit share identical API paths, a sign they run on the same white-label platform.

Deposits are instant and need only an email. Withdrawals go through risk checks, KYC tiers, manual review and, for big wins, a cold-wallet refill, and the terms add weekly caps, instalments and confiscation clauses. Most operate on Curaçao or Anjouan licences costing tens of thousands a year, and reach the US through sweepstakes sister sites that states are now banning.

What to take from this

The edge is the business

Rewards, affiliates, bonuses and marketing are all priced as a share of house edge × volume. Model that first; the site is a wrapper around it.

Own the margin, rent the rest

The big operators build the wallet, originals and rewards engine and rent slots, KYC, sportsbook and support. Start rented, bring pieces in-house as volume grows.

Trust is the moat

The failures in this dossier (unpaid winnings, hot-wallet hacks, regulator exits) are trust failures. Fast payouts and published odds are features.
